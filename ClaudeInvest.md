#【Prompt 4】Backtest a Strategy, But Don’t Fool Yourself

"Use [code] to backtest the 'profit-after-misplacement' strategy with 10 years of daily bar data.

Rules: Buy on the 2nd trading day after any drop of 5% or more related to profit-taking; the prerequisite is that both confirmation days after the drop close above the low of the drop day. Exit after 30 trading days, or exit if the daily bar closes below the drop day's low, whichever comes first. Position size: equal USD amount per trade.

Before running, first tell me: What is this strategy actually betting on? In what market conditions will it fail? State the assumptions upfront so I can see if you make sense later.

Then run the backtest and give me:
· Number of trades (if fewer than 30, just say it’s not statistically significant)
· Win rate, average profit, average loss, profit-loss ratio
· CAGR, maximum drawdown, Sharpe ratio
· The 5 worst trades, with dates and what was happening in the market at the time
· Longest losing streak
· Comparison of the same code during the period vs. buy-and-hold
· One-sided 5 basis point slippage

Finally, answer three questions:
1. Is this a real edge, or just noise?
2. What kind of market condition fed this edge? Does today look like that kind of environment?
3. If I go live with this tomorrow, what’s the most likely thing that’ll disappoint me in the first 6 months?

Don’t beat around the bush. If it’s curve-fitting or the sample’s too small to judge, just say so."
